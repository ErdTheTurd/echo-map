How it works

Three steps from empty space to estimated reflections — all on your device.

  1. Scan

    Walk the room with LiDAR and RoomPlan, or open the built-in living room demo.

  2. Simulate

    Place a source and listener. Paths and arrival times come from geometry and the speed of sound.

  3. Explore

    Compare heat spots, room stats, and material presets — labeled as estimates throughout.

Start with the room’s shape

On an iPhone or iPad with LiDAR, walk the space and let RoomPlan pick up the walls, floor, ceiling, and furniture. That scan becomes the geometry the simulation runs on.

If your device doesn’t have LiDAR, tap Explore Demo Room. You still get the same 3D view, paths, and estimates, using a built-in living room.

Watch where a sound may bounce

Place a source and, if you want, a listener. Thin lines show the direct path and the first few reflections. Arrival times come from path length and the speed of sound, 343 meters per second.

Heat spots mark where reflected energy may build up. They’re meant for comparing one place in the room with another — not as a calibrated sound-pressure reading.

Room stats, labeled as estimates

Volume, surface area, and RT60 estimates sit in a sheet over the room. RT60 uses the Sabine and Eyring formulas, with mid-band absorption from the materials you choose.

Every results screen carries the same note: simulated from room geometry — not a measured reading.

Change the materials, change the estimate

Walls start as painted drywall, the floor as hardwood, the ceiling as drywall, windows as glass, and furniture as upholstered. Each preset uses a typical published absorption value. Switch one, and the estimates update.

It stays on the iPhone

Sonarlark doesn’t create an account, doesn’t need a network connection for its features, and doesn’t collect data. The camera is used only while you scan a room. Geometry and your material settings stay on the device. Read the privacy policy.

Ready when you are

Sonarlark is coming to the App Store. Until then, the estimates stay honest: geometry-based simulations, not measurements.
